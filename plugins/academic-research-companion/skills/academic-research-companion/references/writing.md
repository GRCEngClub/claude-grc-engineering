# Phase 4: Writing

Goal: a complete draft appropriate to the target output — academic paper, whitepaper, or long-form independent piece. Confirm the target early; it changes structure and voice.

## Workflow

Draft section by section, in this order (not reading order):
1. **Methods** — easiest to write, already designed in Phase 3
2. **Results/findings** — just the facts; interpretation waits
3. **Discussion** — what the findings mean, how they answer the question, limitations, implications
4. **Introduction** — written late, when the argument is known
5. **Literature review section** — condensed from the Phase 2 synthesis
6. **Conclusion, then Abstract last** — the abstract summarizes a finished paper

Work one section per sitting; agree on the section's job before drafting it. This matches an iterative drafting style and prevents the blank-page-whole-paper problem.

## Structural templates

**Empirical paper — IMRaD**: Intro → Methods → Results → Discussion. The default for anything with data.

**Introduction — CARS moves** (Swales): (1) establish the territory: why the area matters; (2) establish the niche: what's missing/contested — this is the Phase 2 gap statement; (3) occupy the niche: "this paper does X, contributes Y." A reader should know the question and the contribution by the end of page 1.

**Conceptual/framework paper**: Intro → background/lit → the framework itself, component by component with justification → application/illustration (cases or worked examples) → discussion/limitations.

**Design-science paper**: Intro → related work → problem & requirements → artifact design → evaluation → discussion.

**Abstract formula** (150–250 words): context (1 sentence) → problem/gap (1–2) → what was done (1–2) → key findings (1–2) → implication (1).

## Academic voice, calibrated

- **Hedge claims to match evidence**: "suggests," "is consistent with," "in this sample" — overclaiming is the most common reviewer complaint. But don't hedge into mush; state the actual finding plainly.
- **Limitations are stated by the author, proudly.** A limitations paragraph that beats reviewers to every weakness signals competence.
- **Every empirical claim carries a citation or the paper's own data.** No orphan claims.
- **Define terms on first use**; contested terms (e.g., "AI governance") need an explicit working definition.
- Citation integrity: cite only sources with `verified` status in the tracker ledger. Cite what the source actually says — when in doubt, re-fetch and re-check. Paraphrase; keep rare direct quotes under 15 words.

For independent/blog-published research, the register can relax (first person, shorter paragraphs, the user's brand voice) but the skeleton — explicit question, transparent method, evidence-matched claims, honest limitations, real citations — does not. That skeleton is what separates independent *research* from a hot take. When drafting in the user's public voice, also apply their 'brand-voice' skill if one exists.

## Revision passes (separate, in order)

1. **Argument pass** — does each section do its job? Does the conclusion answer the introduction's question?
2. **Evidence pass** — check every claim against its citation/data; verify all ledger statuses are `verified`
3. **Clarity pass** — sentence-level editing; kill throat-clearing; one idea per paragraph, first sentence states it
4. **Formatting pass** — venue requirements (word count, citation style, anonymization for blind review)

## Exit criteria for Phase 4

- Complete draft in target format
- All citations verified; reference list generated from the citation manager
- Limitations section present
- Tracker updated: draft version, date, open questions for feedback
