# Phase 6: Publishing

Goal: choose and execute the right outlet. Treat the options as an **escalation ladder** — independent publication first to build a public body of work, conferences/workshops as the bridge into scholarly communities, journals when a piece merits the long game. The same project can climb multiple rungs (blog → workshop paper → journal article), if each step adds substance rather than duplicating.

## Rung 1 — Independent publishing (default first outlet)

Self-publishing research is legitimate when it holds itself to research standards. What separates independent research from content:

- **Full methods transparency** — a skeptic can see exactly what was done and retrace it
- **Verified citations** with a real reference list — link DOIs/URLs
- **Data & code released** (GitHub) wherever possible
- **Versioning** — date it, number it, log substantive changes; never silently rewrite findings
- **A citable, permanent form**: archive the artifact on **Zenodo** (free, gives a DOI, versioned) so others can cite it properly; add a "cite this work" block
- **Limitations stated** like in any paper
- **License** it explicitly (CC BY is the common default for open research)

Formats: long-form article on a platform the user controls (personal site, company research blog, Substack, etc. — ask which, never invent a URL), standalone whitepaper PDF, or a GitHub repo with a research-grade README. A practical pattern: full rigorous artifact on the user's own platform + Zenodo DOI, with LinkedIn (or equivalent) as the distribution layer pointing at it — the post is marketing, the artifact is the research.

**Honest framing**: independently published work is real, citable, and portfolio-building, but it is not peer-reviewed and shouldn't be presented as if it were. Label it "independent research" or "working paper." If the user is building an academic credentials portfolio, independent work demonstrates research capability; peer-reviewed items simply weigh more. Hence the ladder.

## Rung 2 — Conferences & workshops (the bridge)

The natural next step, and friendlier to newcomers than journals:

- **Workshops** (co-located with academic conferences) are the entry point: shorter papers (2–6 pages), work-in-progress welcome, lighter review, real feedback, real publication. Search "[topic] workshop CFP [year]."
- **Practitioner conferences** (in the user's domains: BSides, SANS summits, IAPP conferences, ISACA events, local security cons) review talk proposals, not papers — a strong independent piece converts directly into a CFP submission.
- **Academic conferences** (in the user's domains: FAccT, AIES, IEEE S&P and its workshops, HICSS, regional IS conferences) are full peer review with fixed deadline cycles — track CFP deadlines in the tracker, they are unforgiving.
- **Posters and doctoral/student/early-career tracks** are low-barrier rungs within academic conferences; recommend them when the user is eligible (student, early-career, or otherwise qualifies under the CFP).

Verify current CFP details live (web_search) before recommending any specific deadline or venue — never quote a deadline from memory.

## Rung 3 — Journals (the long game)

- **Selecting**: fit beats prestige. Find journals that published the papers in the Phase 2 ledger — the reference list is a venue map. Check scope statements, recent tables of contents, review-time statistics.
- **Predatory-journal screen is mandatory**: run Think. Check. Submit. on any unfamiliar journal, especially ones that email solicitations. Fee-for-fast-review venues damage a CV; they don't build it.
- **Open access economics**: gold OA charges APCs (often $1–3k+, sometimes waivable — ask); subscription journals are free to publish in but paywalled; many subscription journals permit **green OA** self-archiving of the accepted manuscript — check the policy (Sherpa Romeo) so the work can still be publicly posted.
- **Mechanics**: one journal at a time (simultaneous submission is prohibited); tailor formatting; write a short cover letter stating the contribution and fit; expect months per round (see `feedback-and-review.md` for review handling).

## Rung 0.5 — Preprints (a parallel move, not a rung)

Post to arXiv (note: first-time submitters in most categories need an **endorsement** from an established arXiv author — a collaborator or community contact can provide it), SSRN (no endorsement barrier; strong for policy/governance), OSF Preprints, or TechRxiv. Preprints timestamp priority, gather feedback, and coexist with later journal submission at most venues — but check each journal's preprint policy before posting if a specific journal is the target.

## Choosing, concretely

Ask: (1) Who must this reach, and where do they read? (2) What does the user need from it — feedback, credential, reach, speed? (3) What's the realistic quality tier of this piece today? Then pick the rung. A common default when the user wants public impact without waiting on peer review: publish the rigorous independent version now, submit a derived piece to a workshop or practitioner CFP, and reserve journal submission for the strongest one or two projects per year — adjust if their goal is credentials-first or journal-only.

## Exit criteria for Phase 6

- Venue path chosen and logged with reasoning
- For independent: published, archived with DOI, versioned, license attached
- For venue submission: CFP/scope verified live, deadline in tracker, formatting requirements captured
- Post-publication: distribution plan (posts, community shares) and a note of what feedback arrives — it feeds follow-up studies
